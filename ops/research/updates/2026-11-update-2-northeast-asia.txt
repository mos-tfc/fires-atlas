Northeast Asia Artillery and Fire Support
Update (1 May – 24 November 2026)

This reference update records open-source artillery and fire-support developments for China,
Japan, North Korea, and South Korea between 1 May 2026 and 24 November 2026. Across all
four armies, recent activity focused on extending tube, rocket, and stand-off missile reach,
automating fire control, and fielding layered counter-drone defences around firing positions. Two
factual corrections apply to China's existing baseline entries: the Strategic Support Force was
disbanded on 19 April 2024, and the Victory Day Parade featuring the PHL-16 rocket launcher
took place on 3 September 2025 rather than 3 September 2026.
Every entry draws solely on open sources within the stated period, except under Corrections
where confirmed earlier facts rectify existing records. Lines resting on press, state-media,
manufacturer, or single-source claims begin with "Reported:". Where intelligence agencies
disagree—notably on North Korean shell and troop transfers to Russia—each figure is listed
with its own source.
China

Reporting period: 1 May 2026 to 24 November 2026.

1. Equipment

 ● Reported: 22 November 2026 — The People's Liberation Army (PLA) has deployed at
 least two high-power microwave (HPM) air-defence systems to counter small first-person
 view (FPV) drones and swarms. Named directed-energy and kinetic counter-drone
 equipment includes the Norinco OW5-A50 and CASIC LW-30 lasers, the CASIC FK-4000
 HPM system, and programmable airburst ammunition releasing 152 tungsten-alloy
 sub-projectiles. The PLA is also evaluating cable-cutting lasers, bladed interceptor UAVs,
 and rotating wire barriers against fibre-optic guided drones. Source: How is the PLA
 countering small FPV drones and their swarms?, South China Morning Post, 22
 November 2026,
 https://amp.scmp.com/news/china/military/article/3375313/how-pla-countering-small-fpv-d
 rones-and-their-swarms.
 ● Reported: 18 November 2026 — A PLA Army artillery regiment fielded an intelligent
 artillery calibration robot nicknamed the "robot squad leader" to improve technical gunnery
 accuracy on high-altitude plateaus. Developed with manufacturer researchers after July
 2025 and loaded with more than 3,000 cold, high-wind, high-altitude datasets, the system
 automates pre-firing technical inspections and builds per-gun ballistic databases.
 Accompanying state-media imagery showed a PLZ-89 self-propelled howitzer in live-fire
 training. Source: PLA Army develops artillery calibration robot to improve firing accuracy
 on plateaus, Global Times (citing PLA Daily), 18 November 2026,
 https://www.globaltimes.cn/page/202611/1374569.shtml.
 ● Reported: 19 July 2026 — China Military Online and the Ministry of National Defense
 released footage of a PLA Ground Force artillery unit conducting live-fire training with a
 PHL-191 modular rocket launcher delivering airburst fragmentation detonations. Press
 visual analysis assessed the two four-round pods as the 370mm rocket configuration
 (reported range 280–350 km). Official sources did not disclose the rocket designation,
 fuze mechanism, firing range, or measured accuracy. Source: China’s PHL-191 MLRS
 Shows How Airburst Munitions Could Transform Modern Battlefield Suppression, Army
 Recognition (citing China Military Online / Ministry of National Defense), 19 July 2026,
 https://www.armyrecognition.com/news/army-news/2026/chinas-phl-191-mlrs-shows-how-
 airburst-munitions-could-transform-modern-battlefield-suppression.
 ● Reported: 12 May 2026 — State television (CCTV-7) broadcast footage of a PGZ-09
 self-propelled anti-aircraft gun battery from the 16th Heavy Combined Arms Regiment, 6th
 Combined Arms Division (Xinjiang Military District) firing a new 35mm round at target
 drones simulating cruise missiles. The barrels lacked muzzle-mounted electromagnetic
 induction coils, leading U.S. Army University analysts to assess the projectile as an
 armour-piercing discarding sabot (APDS) or similar kinetic round. The round is assessed
 to address thick-cased precision glide bombs such as JDAM-LR that standard AHEAD
 airburst sub-projectiles struggle to penetrate. Source: Air Defense Live-Fire Exercise
 Indicates the PLAA Is Focusing on Destroying New Air Threats, Army University (China
 Landpower Studies Center, citing 12 May 2026 CCTV broadcast), 2026,
 https://www.army.edu/News/View/Article/4580220/air-defense-live-fire-exercise-indicates-t

5. Operations and lessons

 ● 7 October 2026 — A Center for Strategic and International Studies (CSIS) review of
 Chinese military literature found that the PLA National Defense University Joint
 Operations College studies Russia's three-to-five-minute target-to-strike cycle, in which
 Orlan-10 reconnaissance drones cue Kub and Lancet loitering munitions. Rather than
 copying foreign airframes, PLA planners prioritise wiring drones directly into artillery and
 command networks while treating electronic warfare as a deciding factor. The review
 noted that China runs dedicated drone-swarm versus counter-drone trials and feeds
 defensive requirements from Ukrainian strikes directly into domestic manufacturing.
 Source: Learning Without Bleeding: China’s Lessons from the Russia-Ukraine Drone War,
 Center for Strategic and International Studies (CSIS), 7 October 2026,
 https://www.csis.org/analysis/learning-without-bleeding-chinas-lessons-russia-ukraine-dro
 ne-war.

5. Operations and lessons

 ● November 2026 — Two analytical reviews examined KPA operational learning in
 Russia's Kursk region, identifying battlefield adaptation in small-unit dispersion,
 concealment, counter-drone survival, and drone-supported artillery reconnaissance and
 fire coordination. Both studies caution that flat Ukrainian terrain differs sharply from the
 mountainous Korean Peninsula, denser force ratios, and local electromagnetic conditions.
 Institutional adoption across KPA units at home remains unverified without published
 manual revisions or wider training rollout. Sources: Learning Is Not Lessons: What North
 Korea Takes Home from Ukraine, Defense.info, November 2026,
 https://defense.info/partners-corner/2026/11/learning-is-not-lessons-what-north-korea-take
 s-home-from-ukraine/; Kursk as a Training Ground: What North Korea Learns from
 Russia's War against Ukraine, EPIS Thinktank, 2026,
 https://epis-thinktank.com/publications/kursk-as-a-training-ground-what-north-korea-learn
 s-from-russias-war-against-ukraine.
 ● 9 September 2026 & 7–21 August 2026 — Intelligence assessments published
 diverging totals for North Korean munitions and manpower supporting Russia's war
 against Ukraine. South Korea's Defence Intelligence Agency estimated more than 16,000
 KPA personnel sent since October 2024 (including roughly 1,000 demining engineers)
 and more than 15 million artillery rounds supplied, whereas Ukraine's military intelligence
 reported 4 million artillery shells delivered since mid-2023 excluding mortar rounds, 148
 KN-23 and KN-24 ballistic missiles supplied by early 2025, and roughly 25,000 KPA
 personnel rotated in total (with 8,500 on 21 August to 9,500 on 7 August remaining in
 Kursk outside direct operations in Ukraine). Chatham House also cited Ukrainian
 assessments that North Korean ballistic missile accuracy narrowed from 1–3 km early in
 the war to 50–100 m by early 2025, likely reflecting battlefield feedback, improved
 targeting data, or Russian guidance components. Sources: North Korea's military
 partnership with Russia has consequences far beyond Ukraine, Chatham House, 9
