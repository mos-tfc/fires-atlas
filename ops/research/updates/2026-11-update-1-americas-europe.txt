Artillery and Fire Support Reference
Update 1 of 3: United States, Russia and
Ukraine (1 May 2026 to 24 November
2026)

This reference update records confirmed open-source developments in artillery, rocket and
missile fires, fire control, unmanned strike integration, organisation, doctrine, exercises and
combat lessons for the United States, Russia and Ukraine between 1 May 2026 and 24
November 2026. Previously recorded entries and news items are omitted unless materially
updated. Lines resting on press, vendor, state-media or single-source claims begin with
Reported:.
Across the reporting window, three operational trends stand out. The United States expanded
long-range rocket production and ammunition plant modernisation while a Government
Accountability Office audit warned that munitions growth lacked a requirement-linked strategy.
Russia scaled its Rubicon unmanned centre and continued deliveries of Krasnopol-M2 guided
shells and Planshet fire control systems while stockpiling newly built wheeled howitzers near
Finland. Ukraine sustained land-attack Neptune cruise missile strikes at 170–430 km against
Russian airfields and defence plants, integrating Navy missile units into deep joint targeting.

5. Operations and lessons

2026-11-19 — The U.S. Government Accountability Office (GAO) publicly released Report
GAO-27-109420, an unclassified version of its 24 September 2026 classified audit, finding that
the 2026 conflict with Iran reinforced persistent Department of Defense munitions shortages.
GAO reported that DoD lacks an overarching plan linking production and funding to total
requirements, lacks a single empowered implementing office and fails to reconcile its Munitions
Readiness Initiative tool regularly with service databases. The audit cited the U.S. Army's
155mm shell production ramp-up as reactive to partner-support shortfalls rather than driven by
DoD's total munitions requirement; DoD generally concurred with all four recommendations.
Source: DOD Munitions: Planning and Leadership Needed to Address Persistent Shortages,
U.S. Government Accountability Office (GAO-27-109420), 19 November 2026,
https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-27-109420.
Reported: 2026-09-14 — An operational review of missile warfare in the 2026 U.S.–Iran conflict
highlighted the Precision Strike Missile (PrSM) as expanding mobile ground-launched reach to
roughly 500 km with two missiles per HIMARS or M270A2 pod against time-sensitive
air-defence and missile sites. U.S. Central Command stated that PrSM made its combat debut
during opening strikes on 28 February–1 March 2026 (prior to this reporting period), though an
open-source Hague Code of Conduct (HCoC) assessment noted that CENTCOM promotional
video clips included ATACMS M57A1 launch imagery and lacked launch-site or impact
verification. Combat consumption during the conflict contributed to accelerated PrSM production
funding. Sources: US-Iran war ushers in new era of missile warfare, The Express Tribune
(Anadolu Agency), 14 September 2026,
https://tribune.com.pk/story/2629251/us-iran-war-ushers-in-new-era-of-missile-warfare; First use
of the Precision Strike Missile (PrSM) – 2026 Iran war, Hague Code of Conduct (HCoC), date
not stated on page,
https://www.nonproliferation.eu/hcoc/first-use-of-the-precision-strike-missile-prsm-2026-iran-war/
.

2026-06-12 — A Foreign Policy Research Institute (FPRI) study documented that Russia's
Center for Advanced Unmanned Technologies "Rubicon" expanded from 1,450 personnel in

3. Doctrine, training and schools

Reported: 2026-10-04 — For Exercise Zentr-2026, the Russian Ministry of Defence deliberately
selected units without prior combat experience in Ukraine and required them to execute all tasks
using combat methods derived from the Ukraine war. Exercise scenarios integrated dense
UAV-supported fires and formally incorporated the concept of a "security zone in adjacent
territories" into regular manoeuvre schedules. Source: “Zentr-2026” Maneuvers: Putin
Showcases His New Arsenal, Militär Aktuell, 4 October 2026,
https://militaeraktuell.at/en/zentr-2026-maneuvers-putin-showcases-his-new-arsenal/.
2026-06-12 — FPRI documented that the Rubicon Centre trains conventional Russian units in
updated unmanned tactics, techniques and procedures (TTPs) and counter-UAS methods.
Unlike regular formations, Rubicon detachments receive systems directly from manufacturers
and provide direct operational feedback to shorten hardware and software iteration cycles.
Source: Inside Rubicon: The Structure of Russia’s Elite Drone Center, Foreign Policy Research
Institute, 12 June 2026,
https://www.fpri.org/article/2026/06/inside-rubicon-the-structure-of-russias-elite-drone-center/.
